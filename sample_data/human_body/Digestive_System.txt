The digestive system breaks down food so the body can use it. Digestion begins in the mouth. The teeth chew the food, and saliva from the salivary glands starts to break it down. An adult has 32 teeth, and a child has 20 baby teeth. The tongue helps to mix the food and push it into the throat. The esophagus is a muscular tube that carries food from the throat to the stomach. The stomach is a J-shaped organ that mixes food with strong acid and enzymes. The small intestine is about six meters long, and most of the digestion and absorption of food happens there. The large intestine is shorter and wider, and it absorbs water from the leftover food. The liver is the largest internal organ, and it makes bile that helps to digest fats. The liver also cleans the blood and stores energy. The gallbladder stores bile until it is needed. The pancreas makes enzymes for digestion and makes insulin to control the sugar in the blood. The appendix is a small pouch attached to the large intestine. Food takes about 24 to 72 hours to pass through the whole digestive system.
