The five senses are sight, hearing, smell, taste and touch. The eyes are the organs of sight. The pupil is the black circle in the middle of the eye that lets light in. The iris is the colored part of the eye, and it controls the size of the pupil. The lens focuses light onto the retina at the back of the eye. The retina has cells that turn light into signals, and the optic nerve carries these signals to the brain. The ears are the organs of hearing and balance. The ear is divided into the outer ear, the middle ear and the inner ear. The eardrum is a thin skin that vibrates when sound reaches it. The cochlea is a snail-shaped part of the inner ear that turns vibrations into nerve signals. The nose is the organ of smell, and it also warms and cleans the air that we breathe. The tongue is the main organ of taste, and it is covered in small bumps called taste buds. The tongue can sense sweet, sour, salty, bitter and umami tastes. The skin is the organ of touch, and it is the largest organ of the body. The skin protects the body from germs, keeps water in and helps to control body temperature. Skin has three layers: the epidermis, the dermis and the hypodermis. Melanin is the pigment that gives skin its color.
