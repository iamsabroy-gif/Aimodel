The heart is a muscular organ that pumps blood around the body. The heart is about the size of a closed fist, and it is found in the chest between the lungs, slightly to the left. The heart has four chambers: the left atrium, the right atrium, the left ventricle and the right ventricle. The atria receive blood, and the ventricles pump blood out. The heart has four valves that keep the blood flowing in one direction. A healthy adult heart beats about 60 to 100 times a minute. The heart beats about 100,000 times a day. Arteries carry blood away from the heart. Veins carry blood back to the heart. Capillaries are the tiniest blood vessels, and they connect arteries to veins. The aorta is the largest artery in the body. The left side of the heart pumps oxygen-rich blood to the body, and the right side pumps blood to the lungs to pick up oxygen. An adult has about five liters of blood. Blood is made of red blood cells, white blood cells, platelets and plasma. Red blood cells carry oxygen, and they contain a protein called haemoglobin that makes blood red. White blood cells fight germs and infections. Platelets help the blood to clot when you have a cut. Plasma is the yellowish liquid that carries the blood cells. The four main blood groups are A, B, AB and O.
