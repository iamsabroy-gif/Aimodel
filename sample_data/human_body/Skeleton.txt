The skeleton is the frame of the human body. An adult skeleton has 206 bones. A baby is born with about 300 bones, and many of them join together as the child grows. The skull protects the brain. The skull is made of 22 bones, and the lower jaw, called the mandible, is the only skull bone that moves. The spine, also called the backbone, protects the spinal cord. The spine is made of 33 small bones called vertebrae. The rib cage has 12 pairs of ribs, which protect the heart and the lungs. The breastbone, also called the sternum, is in the middle of the chest and joins the ribs at the front. The femur is the thigh bone, and it is the longest and strongest bone in the body. The smallest bone in the body is the stapes, which is found inside the ear. The humerus is the bone of the upper arm. The radius and the ulna are the two bones of the forearm. The tibia is the shin bone, and the fibula is the thinner bone next to it. The patella, also called the kneecap, protects the knee joint. Each hand has 27 bones, and each foot has 26 bones. A joint is a place where two bones meet. The knee and the elbow are hinge joints, and the shoulder and the hip are ball-and-socket joints. Ligaments are tough bands that connect one bone to another bone. Cartilage is a smooth, flexible tissue that cushions the joints. Bones store calcium and make blood cells in the soft bone marrow inside them. The pelvis is the ring of bones at the base of the spine, and it supports the weight of the upper body.
