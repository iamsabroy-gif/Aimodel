The lungs are the organs that let the body breathe. A person has two lungs, one on each side of the chest, and they are protected by the rib cage. The left lung is slightly smaller than the right lung to leave room for the heart. The right lung has three lobes, and the left lung has two lobes. When you breathe in, air enters through the nose or the mouth and passes through the throat to the windpipe. The windpipe is also called the trachea. The trachea splits into two tubes called bronchi, and one bronchus goes to each lung. Inside the lungs the bronchi divide into smaller tubes called bronchioles. At the ends of the bronchioles are tiny air sacs called alveoli. Oxygen passes from the alveoli into the blood, and carbon dioxide passes from the blood into the alveoli to be breathed out. The lungs contain about 300 million alveoli. The diaphragm is the muscle under the lungs that moves down when you breathe in. An adult at rest breathes about 12 to 20 times a minute. The epiglottis is a small flap that stops food from going into the windpipe when you swallow. The voice box, also called the larynx, contains the vocal cords that make sound.
