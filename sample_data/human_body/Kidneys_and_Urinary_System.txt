The kidneys are two bean-shaped organs that clean the blood. They are each about the size of a fist, and they are found at the back of the abdomen, one on each side of the spine. The kidneys filter waste and extra water from the blood to make urine. Every day the kidneys filter about 180 liters of blood. Urine travels from each kidney down a tube called the ureter to the bladder. The bladder is a stretchy bag that stores urine until you go to the toilet. The urethra is the tube that carries urine out of the body. The kidneys also help to control blood pressure and the balance of salts in the body. A person can live a normal life with only one healthy kidney. The adrenal glands sit on top of the kidneys and make hormones such as adrenaline. The thyroid is a butterfly-shaped gland in the neck that controls how fast the body uses energy. The pituitary gland is a pea-sized gland at the base of the brain, and it is called the master gland because it controls other glands. The spleen is an organ on the left side of the abdomen that filters old blood cells and helps to fight infection.
