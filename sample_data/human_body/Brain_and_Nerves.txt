The brain is the control center of the human body. An adult brain weighs about 1.4 kilograms and contains about 86 billion nerve cells called neurons. The brain is protected by the skull. The brain has three main parts: the cerebrum, the cerebellum and the brainstem. The cerebrum is the largest part of the brain, and it is used for thinking, memory, speech and movement. The cerebellum is at the back of the brain, and it controls balance and coordination. The brainstem connects the brain to the spinal cord, and it controls breathing and the heartbeat. The brain is divided into a left half and a right half called hemispheres. The spinal cord is a long bundle of nerves that runs down the spine and carries messages between the brain and the body. Nerves are like wires that carry messages as tiny electrical signals. The brain and the spinal cord together make up the central nervous system. The nerves that spread through the rest of the body make up the peripheral nervous system. Reflexes are quick actions that the body does without waiting for the brain, like pulling your hand away from something hot. The brain uses about 20 percent of the energy of the body.
