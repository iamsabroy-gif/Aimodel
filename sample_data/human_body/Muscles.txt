Muscles are soft tissues that contract to make the body move. The human body has more than 600 muscles. There are three kinds of muscle: skeletal muscle, smooth muscle and cardiac muscle. Skeletal muscles are attached to the bones and move the body when you choose to move. Smooth muscles are found in the walls of organs such as the stomach and the blood vessels, and they work without you thinking about them. Cardiac muscle is found only in the heart, and it never gets tired. Tendons are strong cords that connect muscles to bones. The Achilles tendon joins the calf muscle to the heel bone, and it is the thickest tendon in the body. The biceps is the muscle on the front of the upper arm that bends the elbow. The triceps is the muscle on the back of the upper arm that straightens the elbow. The quadriceps is the group of four muscles on the front of the thigh. The hamstrings are the muscles on the back of the thigh. The gluteus maximus is the largest muscle in the body, and it forms most of the buttock. The diaphragm is a large dome-shaped muscle under the lungs that helps you breathe. The smallest muscle in the body is the stapedius, which is found in the ear. Muscles work in pairs, so when one muscle contracts the other relaxes. Exercise makes muscles stronger.
