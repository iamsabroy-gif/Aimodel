A bicycle is a human-powered vehicle with two wheels attached to a frame, one behind the other. The rider sits on a saddle and pushes pedals with their feet. The pedals turn a chain that drives the rear wheel. Bicycles were invented in the early 19th century in Germany, and the first pedal-driven version appeared in the 1860s in France. Today more than one billion bicycles are in use around the world. They are the main means of transport in many cities, especially in the Netherlands and Denmark. The brakes slow the wheels by pressing rubber pads against the rims. Most bicycles have gears that make it easier to climb hills. A typical road bicycle weighs about nine kilograms, and racing bicycles are made of carbon fiber to keep them light. Cycling is good exercise because it strengthens the legs and the heart. The Tour de France is the most famous cycle race, and it is held every July.
