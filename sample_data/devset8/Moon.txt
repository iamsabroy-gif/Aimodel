The Moon is Earth's only natural satellite. It orbits the Earth at an average distance of about 384,000 kilometers. The Moon takes about 27 days to go around the Earth once. Its diameter is about 3,474 kilometers, which is a little more than a quarter of the diameter of Earth. The gravity on the Moon is about one sixth of the gravity on Earth, so astronauts can jump very high there. The Moon has no air and no liquid water on its surface. The dark patches that can be seen from Earth are called maria, which is Latin for seas. The surface is covered in craters that were made by asteroids hitting it. The Moon causes the tides in the oceans of the Earth. Neil Armstrong was the first person to walk on the Moon, in July 1969, during the Apollo 11 mission. Twelve people have walked on it in total.
